Artifact 2

Machine Learning vs. Deep Learning Industry Analysis

View Full Artifact

Introduction

This collaborative presentation analyzes how machine learning and deep learning are transforming retail and e-commerce. It compares traditional machine learning for structured demand forecasting with deep learning for unstructured visual search.

Objective

The objective was to determine when retailers should use traditional machine learning and when deep learning is the stronger choice. The analysis evaluates data type, model complexity, interpretability, computing cost, training requirements, business value, and ethical risk.

My Role and Contributions

I served as the Comparativist and Strategist. I led the head-to-head comparison, evaluated whether the approaches could be exchanged across problems, analyzed cost and explainability trade-offs, developed the recommended AI adoption roadmap, and presented the ethical reflection.

Industry Applications

Walmart demand forecasting illustrates how tree-based machine learning supports structured inventory decisions. Alibaba Pailitao visual search illustrates why convolutional neural networks are better suited to complex image data at billion-image scale.

Key Finding

The right algorithm is the one the data demands. Traditional machine learning is the stronger starting point for structured retail data because it is efficient, explainable, and economical. Deep learning earns its additional cost when value is hidden in complex unstructured data such as images.

Professional Value

This artifact demonstrates comparative technical analysis, evidence-based model selection, business strategy, AI adoption planning, cost and explainability evaluation, collaborative presentation, and responsible AI decision-making.

Artifact 3(ii)

Communicating for Learning: Neural Networks

View Full Artifact

Introduction

“Neural Networks: A Team of Digital Investigators” is a beginner-friendly explanation of how neural networks learn from examples, examine information through layers, adjust weights, correct errors, and make predictions. The activity uses email filtering as a relatable real-world example.

Objective

The objective was to communicate an important AI/ML concept accurately to a nontechnical audience by limiting unnecessary terminology, introducing ideas gradually, and using a professional analogy that makes layers, weights, training, and prediction easier to understand.

Communication Approach

I compared a neural network to a team of digital investigators. The input layer receives evidence, hidden layers examine clues and combine patterns, and the output layer produces a prediction. The explanation also addresses training-data quality, possible classification errors, and the continuing need for human oversight.

AI Use & Revision

I used ChatGPT to brainstorm and simplify the initial explanation, then refined the prompt for a beginner audience, a relatable analogy, limited technical terminology, and a practical email-filtering example. I reviewed the result for accuracy and revised it to reflect my own understanding and communication style.

Professional Value

This artifact demonstrates audience-centered technical writing, neural-network knowledge, prompt refinement, critical review, responsible AI disclosure, and the ability to translate complex concepts into clear professional communication.

Artifact 4

Navigating Human Bias in AI and Data Analytics Leadership

View Full Artifact

Introduction

This reflective leadership analysis examines how human assumptions, cognitive biases, organizational practices, and historical data can shape AI-assisted decisions. It establishes responsible inclusion as my central leadership value: technology should expand human opportunity rather than automate existing inequalities.

Objective

The objective was to critically examine my own potential biases, identify where unfairness can enter the AI lifecycle, and develop practical safeguards for problem definition, data collection, model evaluation, organizational adoption, post-deployment monitoring, and incident response.

Process

I examined confirmation, affinity, anchoring, status quo, groupthink, and automation biases; connected them to real AI and data-analytics decisions; and developed a five-stage leadership framework focused on examining assumptions, improving representation, measuring fairness, engaging stakeholders, and maintaining governance.

Tools & Methods

The artifact combines first-person critical reflection, AI-lifecycle analysis, fairness measures such as false positives, false negatives, precision, and recall, structured decision records, controlled pilots, bias audits, human review, appeals, and transparent corrective action. ChatGPT supported brainstorming and refinement, followed by personal review for accuracy, authenticity, and alignment with my values.

Value Proposition

This artifact demonstrates my ability to unite AI/ML knowledge with ethical reasoning, inclusive change leadership, risk management, professional communication, and governance practices that protect the people affected by automated decisions.
